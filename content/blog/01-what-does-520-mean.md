# What Does 520 Mean? The Complete Guide to Chinese Internet Love Slang

**520 means “I love you” in Chinese internet slang.** Spoken aloud, the digits 5-2-0 (wǔ èr líng) sound similar to 我爱你 (wǒ ài nǐ), which translates to “I love you” in Mandarin. Because of this, May 20 (5/20) has become an unofficial online Valentine's Day in China, when couples exchange gifts, red envelopes, and declarations of love.

If you just received a “520” text — congratulations, someone loves you. Here's everything behind those three digits.

## Where Did 520 Come From?

Number-based slang thrives in Chinese because Mandarin has a huge number of homophones — words that sound alike but mean different things. Typing numbers was faster than typing characters in the pager and early-SMS era of the 1990s, so numeric codes stuck.

The love connection was cemented by singer Fan Xiaolan's 1998 song *“Digital Love”* (数字恋爱), which explicitly mapped 520 to 我爱你. From there, chat rooms, QQ, and later WeChat spread it to hundreds of millions of users. Today the [Chinese internet slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Internet_slang) ecosystem includes hundreds of number codes, but 520 remains the most famous.

## How 520 Day Is Celebrated on May 20

May 20 has grown into a full commercial and cultural event, often called “Online Valentine's Day” (网络情人节):

- **Confessions (表白):** May 20 is considered the day to confess feelings — many couples mark it as their anniversary.
- **Red envelopes:** Sending ¥5.20 or ¥52.00 via WeChat is a classic micro-gift meaning “I love you.”
- **Marriage registrations:** Chinese civil-affairs offices see registration spikes every May 20; many offices extend hours for the rush.
- **Gifts and dates:** Flowers, jewelry, and — increasingly — personalized digital gifts like [AI love letters](/en/ai-love-letter) and [AI couple portraits](/en/ai-couple-portrait).

## How Should You Reply to 520?

If someone sends you 520 and you feel the same way, the classic reply is **521** (“I'm willing / I love you too”). Other common responses:

- **5201314** — I love you forever (raising the stakes)
- **2520** — 爱我爱你 style wordplay meaning “love me, love you”
- **A gift back** — many couples answer a 520 message with a personalized creation, like a custom [AI love song](/en/ai-love-song) written from shared memories

Not feeling it? A friendly deflection like “aww, 谢谢 (thanks)!” keeps things light without leading anyone on.

China effectively celebrates love several times a year: Western Valentine's Day (Feb 14), 520 Day (May 20), and the traditional Qixi Festival (7th day of the 7th lunar month). Each has its own flavor — 520 is the digital-native one, born entirely from internet culture. ## FAQ

### Does 520 mean “I love you” in Chinese?

Yes. The digits 5-2-0 are pronounced wǔ èr líng in Mandarin, which sounds close to wǒ ài nǐ (我爱你), meaning “I love you.” It's one of the most widely used pieces of Chinese internet slang.

### What is 520 Day?

520 Day falls on May 20 (5/20) each year. It's an unofficial “Online Valentine's Day” in China when couples confess feelings, exchange gifts and red envelopes, and register marriages in record numbers.

### What's the difference between 520 and 521?

520 (May 20) is traditionally the day to say “I love you,” often associated with men confessing. 521 (May 21) sounds like “I'm willing” and is the classic acceptance or reply, often associated with the other partner answering.

### What does 5201314 mean?

5201314 combines 520 (“I love you”) with 1314 (yī sān yī sì, sounding like 一生一世, “a whole lifetime”). Together they mean “I love you forever.”

### How do I respond if someone sends me 520?

Reply 521 (“I'm willing”) or 5201314 (“I love you forever”) if you feel the same. Many people also respond with a personalized gift, such as an AI-written love letter or a custom love song.
