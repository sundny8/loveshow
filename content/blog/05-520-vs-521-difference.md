# 520 vs 521: What's the Difference?

**520 sounds like “I love you” (我爱你, wǒ ài nǐ) in Mandarin and is the day to confess; 521 sounds like “I'm willing” (我愿意, wǒ yuàn yì) and is the day to answer.** In practice, May 20 is when one partner — traditionally the man — declares their love, and May 21 is when the other accepts. Together they form a two-day internet Valentine's celebration in China.

That's the short version. The details — and the modern reality — are more interesting.

## The Homophone Logic

Chinese number slang works by sound-alike. Here's the exact mapping:

| Number | Mandarin sound | Phrase it mimics | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | wǔ | 我 (wǒ) | I / me |
| 2 | èr | 爱 (ài) | love |
| 0 | líng | 你 (nǐ) | you |
| 1 | yī | 意 (yì) | willing (in 愿意) |

So **520** ≈ 我爱你 (“I love you”), while **521** ≈ 我愿意 (“I'm willing / I accept”). Neither is phonetically perfect — Mandarin speakers hear the intent through the approximation, the same way English speakers read “gr8” as “great.” For the full background on number slang, see our [complete guide to 520](/en/blog/what-does-520-mean) or the deep-dive [520 meaning page](/en/520-meaning).

## Two Days, Two Roles

The folk convention that emerged in the 2000s:

- **May 20 (520) — Confession Day.** The initiating partner declares love: messages at 5:20, red envelopes of ¥5.20 or ¥52.00, gifts, dinner reservations.
- **May 21 (521) — Answer Day.** The receiving partner responds. Replying “521” means “I'm willing — I love you too.” Some couples treat 521 as “the day she says it back,” others as the day for the quieter partner to take the stage.

In reality, modern couples mix the two freely — plenty of people send 521 as a standalone “I love you,” and same-sex and long-distance couples remix the tradition however it fits. The two-day structure survives because it's fun: it builds a call-and-response into the calendar.

## Which Date Should You Celebrate?

Practical guidance:

1. **Confessing to someone new?** Use 520 (May 20). It's the canonical confession date and your message needs no explanation.
2. **Replying to a confession?** Send 521 — ideally on May 21 for full tradition points, though same-day replies are common.
3. **Established couple?** Celebrate 520 as the main event; use 521 for a quieter second act (a handwritten note, a home-cooked dinner).
4. **Long-distance?** Split the roles: one partner owns 5/20, the other owns 5/21. Two days of gifts across time zones — ideas in our [long-distance AI gift guide](/en/blog/long-distance-relationship-ai-gift-ideas).

## How the Dates Fit China's Love-Day Calendar

China's romantic calendar now includes Western Valentine's Day (Feb 14), 520/521 (May 20–21), Qixi Festival (7th day of the 7th lunar month, usually August), and Singles' Day (Nov 11) as the anti-love counterweight. The [Qixi Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixi_Festival) carries two millennia of tradition; 520/521 is its digital-native younger sibling. ## FAQ

### What does 521 mean in Chinese?

521 (wǔ èr yī) sounds like 我愿意 (wǒ yuàn yì) — “I'm willing / I accept.” It's the traditional reply to a 520 confession, and many people also use it as a variant of “I love you.”

### Is 520 for men and 521 for women?

That's the folk convention — men confess on 5/20, women answer on 5/21 — but modern usage is flexible. Any partner can use either date; the two-day call-and-response is the part that stuck.

### Can I send 521 without receiving a 520 first?

Yes. 521 works as a standalone “I love you / I'm willing,” and May 21 is a fine day to confess if you missed May 20.

### What's a good 521 reply gift?

Something that answers rather than repeats: a reply letter, a custom song responding to their confession, or a red envelope of ¥5.21. The theme is “you asked, and here is my answer.”

### Are 520 and 521 official holidays?

No — both are grassroots internet traditions, not public holidays. But they're commercially huge in China, with marriage registrations spiking on May 20 each year.
