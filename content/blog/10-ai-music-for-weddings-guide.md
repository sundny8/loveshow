# AI Music for Weddings: A Practical Guide

AI music works best at weddings in three spots: **a custom first-dance song written about your relationship, a personalized processional, and one surprise dedication track during the reception.** It doesn't replace your DJ or band — it adds the one thing no playlist can: songs that literally contain your names, your story, and your vows. Here's exactly how to plan, generate, and play AI music at a real wedding without technical or emotional missteps.

## Where AI Music Fits (and Where It Doesn't)

| Wedding moment | AI music fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First dance | Excellent | A song about *you two* — the emotional core |
| Processional / aisle | Excellent | Instrumental version of “your song” |
| Vow backing track | Good | Soft instrumental under spoken vows |
| Surprise dedication | Excellent | Groom/bride surprise mid-reception |
| Parent dances | Good | Lyrics can honor a parent by name |
| Dance floor sets | Poor | Crowds want familiar hits — keep the DJ |
| Cocktail hour | Good | Custom instrumental background loops |

The pattern: AI shines in **story moments**, not party moments. Guests dance to songs they know; they cry at songs about people they know.

## The Planning Timeline

1. **8+ weeks out — write your brief.** Collect your story: how you met, proposal details, phrases from your vows. The same memory-mining method from our [love letter guide](/en/blog/how-to-write-love-letter-with-ai) applies to lyrics.
2. **6 weeks — generate and shortlist.** Create 3–5 candidates with the [AI love song generator](/en/ai-love-song). Vary genre and tempo; shortlist two.
3. **4 weeks — test the dance.** Actually dance to your first-dance candidate. Check length (2.5–3.5 minutes is ideal — cut longer tracks) and tempo.
4. **2 weeks — deliver files to your DJ/venue.** Provide WAV or high-bitrate MP3, labeled clearly, with cue notes (“fade in at 0:08”). Do a soundcheck on the venue system if possible.
5. **Day of — have a backup.** Phone + cable + the file offline. Never depend on venue Wi-Fi.

## Writing the First-Dance Song

The brief matters more than the model. Include:

- Both names (and how you actually say them — nicknames count)
- Two or three concrete story beats: the city you met in, the disaster date, the proposal line
- The genre you both actually listen to — not what sounds “wedding-appropriate”
- Explicit tempo guidance: “slow ballad, danceable at 70–80 BPM”

## The Surprise Dedication (Handle with Care)

A mid-reception surprise song — groom to bride, couple to parents — is the highest-impact AI music moment. Three rules:

1. **Keep it under 3 minutes.** Surprise attention spans are short.
2. **Print the lyrics** on cards or screens; first-time listeners catch maybe 60% of sung words.
3. **Tell the DJ, tell no one else.** The DJ needs the file and the cue; everyone else needs the surprise.

## Etiquette and Practicalities

- **Licensing:** AI platforms vary on commercial-use terms, but private wedding playback is personal use on essentially all mainstream services — check your plan if the wedding is being professionally filmed for public distribution.
- **Tell your videographer** which tracks are AI-generated originals; it saves them music-licensing headaches on the wedding film, a real advantage over copyrighted hits (background on how [music licensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_licensing) complicates wedding videos).
- **Don't over-AI.** One to three custom moments is moving; an all-AI wedding soundtrack exhausts the novelty and the guests.
- **Match the vocal style** to your culture and venue acoustics — big-hall reverb favors slower, cleaner arrangements.

## FAQ

### Can I use an AI-generated song for my first dance?

Yes — it's the single best use of AI music at a wedding. Generate it 6–8 weeks ahead, keep it 2.5–3.5 minutes, and practice dancing to it before the day.

### Is AI wedding music legal to play?

Playing AI-generated tracks at a private event is personal use under mainstream platforms' terms. If your wedding film will be published commercially, check your generation plan's license — AI originals are typically easier to clear than copyrighted songs.

### What should the lyrics include?

Both your names, two or three specific story beats (how you met, the proposal), and optionally a line from your vows. Specificity is what makes guests reach for tissues.

### Should AI music replace our wedding DJ?

No. AI music excels at story moments — first dance, processional, dedications. Dance floors run on familiar hits, which is exactly what DJs are for.

### What file format should I give the venue?

WAV or 320 kbps MP3, delivered to the DJ at least two weeks early, clearly labeled with cue notes — and carry an offline backup on your phone.
